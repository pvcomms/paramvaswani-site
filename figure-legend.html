Reading the Figures

What each diagram claims, what every moving thing means, and why five formal lenses on one person add up to a single argument rather than a pile of visualizations.

fig. 1

What coexists

set theory · the container

Identity isn’t a list of interests. It’s an overlap structure — and the interesting parts of a person live in the intersections, not the circles.

Five domains: mind/body, technology, philosophy, society, creation. On their own they’re just topics; a bio could list them. What the diagram argues is that none of the seven things actually worth naming exist inside a single domain. Cognitive architecture only exists where mind meets technology. Phenomenology only where mind meets philosophy. AI alignment where technology meets philosophy. Structural difference where a differently-built mind meets a world built for the default. Power & consent where philosophy meets society. Building your way out where technology meets creation. Making as thinking where creation meets philosophy.

Every label sits on a region that geometrically exists — verified by scanning the actual pixel areas before anything got named. The diagram never claims a region it doesn’t have. That restraint is the epistemics of the page in miniature: a picture that would rather be incomplete than overstate.

𝒰 · both / and
The frame around everything. Both/and isn’t one of the topics — it’s the operator that lets contradictory regions coexist without being resolved. Putting it on the frame instead of at the center is the single most load-bearing decision in the figure.
the red dot, “me”
Sits at mind ∩ technology ∩ philosophy — deliberately not at the center of any one circle. The claim: I’m not any of these fields, I’m the point where three of them cross.
the unlabeled dashed set
A sixth circle, half outside the frame, no name, no hover fill, not clickable. Its only vocabulary is “off the record.” Withholding built into the information architecture rather than announced in prose.
lens shading
Hovering a two-domain region fills the actual overlap shape, not a glow around a word. You see the exact region you’re being told the name of.
click → filter → enter
First click dims the rest and holds a card; second click routes to the part of the site that embodies that domain (mind→thoughts.log, technology→fig. 2, philosophy→cohere, society→fig. 3, creation→the work). The diagram is also the navigation, so the taxonomy has to be true or the site breaks.
what moves, and why The circles draw themselves in at load, staggered over ~2.4s — the identity assembles rather than arriving pre-formed. Bloom on hover is attention: the lit circle gains a glow, the others drop to 12% — thinking about one thing means the rest goes quiet, briefly. Two pale balls drift continuously, taking the color of whatever domain they’re crossing and bouncing off the off-record set: attention wanders on its own schedule, takes on the character of wherever it lands, and doesn’t get into what’s private. The red dot breathes but never travels — the fixed point, alive but not moving. The map is static; the traffic isn’t.
fig. 2

How far from the mean

statistics · the position

Variance is the structure of things, not an error in your model — and individuality isn’t the far tail, it’s choosing your distance from the mean on purpose.

One axis: share of cognition delegated to machines, none to everything. Three fixed marks tell the overcorrection arc — autumn ’25 at the far right (every decision through the machine: efficient, and not entirely mine), spring ’26 at the far left (cold turkey: proved the point, paid in speed), and now, a chosen position right of center, revised monthly. The word overcorrection sits over the swing between the tails — the one place on the page the machine-learning register is allowed to surface twice.

Hovering the bands names them the way a statistician would: the middle is “where most things cluster · base rates matter · the probable”; the tails are “the unexpected · rare events · calibrate for tails.” This is the figure that argues against optimization-as-virtue — the mean is not a target, it’s just where the density is.

the feed’s thumb
The slider biases the whole distribution rightward. The curve slides; the three marks stay pinned. That’s the entire power argument in one gesture: the crowd’s center of mass moves, your own history doesn’t, and the gap between them is what a platform does to a population.
μ fair vs μ actual
Two mean markers. The first is where the mean sits with no thumb on the scale. The second appears only once you’ve pushed it, labeled “the thumb moved everyone.”
±1σ, tracking
The sigma boundaries move with the mean rather than staying fixed — because the reference frame is what shifted, not the numbers.
the machine (second view)
The same claim, proven instead of asserted. A Galton board: nine rows of pegs, every peg a left/right branch, balls piling into the curve. The probability tree and the normal distribution are the same object — one seen before, one after. The slider now biases every individual peg.
the tracked red ball
One ball in each batch is drawn red and trailed. Watch it and you cannot tell the machine is biased — a single path is pure noise. The bias is only legible in the pile. That’s why individual experience can’t settle a question about a system.
what moves, and why The curve view has zero latency by design — drag the thumb and the whole distribution slides in real time, because the claim is about a mechanism, and mechanisms should feel immediate. The machine view is the opposite kind of time: balls fall at their own pace and the pile accumulates over a minute or two, because the point is that lawfulness only emerges with n. Two different speeds carrying two different epistemics — instant for the model, slow for the evidence.
fig. 3

The influence graph

graph theory · the relations

Nothing arrives alone, and every edge runs both ways. Neither the passive-consumer story nor the pure-autonomy story survives contact with the actual graph.

Fig. 1 shows what overlaps; this shows what flows. Eleven nodes around a red center — the feed, the machines, Aeon and Psyche, LessWrong, Kierkegaard, Clark & Chalmers, Whittaker, MUBI, Fleet Foxes, the old ghosts — and the hover captions are written bidirectionally on purpose. “The feed ↔ me: it shaped my taste; my attention paid its rent. Neither of us is innocent.” That sentence is the thesis of the whole figure: influence is a trade, not a transmission.

Nodes connect to each other, not only to the center — the feed touches the old ghosts and the machines; Kierkegaard touches Clark & Chalmers. Being shaped by something means being shaped by everything it’s connected to. The graph is also where the taste history stays legible without being confessed.

what moves, and why The nodes never quite settle — a slow idle wobble, a couple of pixels, never resolving into a fixed arrangement. An influence map that held perfectly still would be claiming the relationships are finished. Hovering a node lights only its own edges: you can trace what one thing touches, but you can’t see the whole graph at once, which is also true of the actual situation.
fig. 4

Precedence

decision theory · the choice

Values don’t rank in the abstract; they rank per situation. A principle you can’t route is a slogan.

A decision tree that’s actually a values router. Does it touch a person → kindness, and the edge is annotated always — the only “always” on the site. Otherwise: can it be undone? If no → uncertainty (one-way doors get a credence first). If yes, is everyone already doing it? Yes → individuality (the mean moved, not the truth). No → taste (when nothing else claims jurisdiction, judgment does). And a separate short branch, is it anyone’s business? → privacy, marked end of route.

The situation chips let you call the router on a real case and watch it resolve edge by edge. This is the figure most exposed to rationalist cringe, so two guards: no probabilities anywhere on it — putting p = .8 on “kindness governs” would be self-parody, and numbers live in the ledger instead — and a footnote that undercuts the whole apparatus: deterministic here. messier in production.

the privacy branch
The route visibly stops early, short of the tree’s full depth. A value you can watch refuse to traverse. It’s the only structural argument on the page that doesn’t need a sentence.
the traveling dot
The same red dot from fig. 1, now walking the tree — identity moving through a decision rather than sitting in a diagram.
cross-references
The uncertainty leaf points to the ledger; the individuality leaf points back to fig. 2. The figures cite each other, like a paper.
fig. 5

Directionally correct

dynamics · the practice

You don’t arrive at truth. You stay pointed at it while a stream of inputs bends your heading — and the honest score is an angle, not a total.

A breakout game where the ball is a belief. Green bricks (evidence, base rate, contra, primary source, steelman, sleep on it) bend its path toward a truth marker; violet bricks (vibes, the feed, consensus, sunk cost, engagement, hot take, clout) bend it away. The paddle is labeled judgment — your only control, and it only gets to choose the return angle. The readout is the live angular error between where the belief is heading and where truth sits, turning gold under 25°: directionally correct.

bricks pass through
They don’t bounce the ball, they bend it. An input doesn’t block a thought; it redirects one. The physics had to be rewritten to make that true.
truth · approx.
The target is labeled approximate and it drifts. Aiming at a moving, uncertain target is the actual condition; a fixed bullseye would be the lie.
the field repopulates
Every twenty seconds, cleared or not. The inputs never stop arriving — there’s no state where you’ve processed everything.
no win screen
There is no score to maximize and nothing to complete. The only failure is dropping the belief entirely, and the caption for that is “belief dropped. click to reopen the question.”
what moves, and why This is the one figure that requires you to participate to say anything. Figs. 1–4 can be read; fig. 5 has to be played, badly, for a minute. That’s the argument: epistemics is a motor skill, not a position you hold.
around the figures

The three layers that hold them

accountability · time · architecture
  • The ledger + the tape. Beliefs with numbers and deltas, and a ticker that quotes them like market prices. The tape is the anti-feed joke made structural: the only auto-scrolling thing on the site scrolls commitments you can be wrong about, not virtues. The last row bets against the site itself — this design survives twelve months, p = .35 ▼.
  • thoughts.log. Dated, tagged, tail -f. The figures show a structure; the log shows it changing. Without it the page would claim to be finished.
  • The four rooms + the nav. Create, consume, curate, cohere — verbs. Fig. 1’s domains are nouns: what I’m made of. The verbs are what I do with them. That’s why both survived the redesign, and why the hero’s circles deep-link into verb-named sections: the nouns route into the doing.
the argument

How five diagrams become one claim

why this isn’t a pile of charts

Each figure is a different formalism aimed at the same person, and none of them is sufficient alone. What persists across all five coordinate systems is the thing the site is actually about.

Set theory can show what coexists but says nothing about degree. Statistics can place you on an axis but flattens you to one number. A graph shows relations but not decisions. A decision tree shows choices but pretends they’re clean. A dynamical system shows the practice but no structure. Run all five and the residue — the red dot that appears in every one — is the argument. A self can be modeled without being reduced, which is the both/and thesis executed rather than stated.

device what it does across every figure
the red dot Same color, size, and breathing in fig. 1 (identity), fig. 2 (position), fig. 4 (walking the tree), fig. 5 (the belief in play). Established once, never explained again. Recurrence does the work silently.
the caption bar One component under every figure — same mono, same height, same em-dash rest state. Three instruments on one panel, not three illustrations in a row.
numbering Figures are numbered like a paper because the page is a paper: an argument with evidence, not a portfolio with decoration.
cross-citation Fig. 2 points forward to fig. 4; fig. 4’s leaves point back to fig. 2 and to the ledger; fig. 3’s rest caption points back to fig. 1. A cycle, not a stack.
one home per value Nuance lives in 𝒰. Individuality lives in the curve. Uncertainty, kindness, taste and privacy are the tree’s leaves. Both/and is the frame. No value is ever listed as signage — that’s why there’s no wall of virtues anywhere.

The negative space is also an argument. Nothing on the page autoplays except one breathing dot and two one-time draw-ons. Nothing records what you do — no analytics, no cookies, no stored input, no counter of your behavior. The page cannot learn about you. On a site whose subject is what feeds do to minds, that restraint is the load-bearing claim: the interaction design has to be able to survive its own critique.

sanity check

Is it actually grounded?

the honest audit

Short answer: one figure is real mathematics, three are honest pictures a viewer will over-read, and one made a claim that was simply false until it got fixed. Here is which is which.

audit.log — written against my own work
fig. 2 · the distributionsolid

The strongest thing on the site, and the only part that would survive a statistician. Binomial paths really do converge to a normal distribution; a per-branch bias really does shift the population mean by n(p−½) × pitch; a single tracked path really is uninformative about the bias. Checked numerically rather than asserted: at p = .65 the simulated mean landed 53.2px from center against a theoretical 54.

the honest caveatThe mechanism is real; the personal axis is illustrative. “Share of cognition delegated to machines” has no units and was never measured. The theorem is true; the three dates on it are a story about a person.

fig. 1 · the vennpartly

What is real: every named intersection is a genuine field that exists at exactly that overlap, and every label sits on a region confirmed to have real area before it was named. What is not real: five overlapping circles cannot represent all thirty-one possible combinations — a hard geometric limit, not an editorial choice. So the absences are artifacts. Society ∩ technology has no region because circles can’t do it, yet a viewer reads that absence as a claim. Circle sizes are decorative too: they encode nothing while looking like they encode magnitude.

what would close itOne line under the figure: “five circles can’t show every overlap; the missing ones are geometry, not opinion.” Cheap, and it converts a hidden weakness into a visible epistemic standard.

fig. 3 · the influence graphwas overclaiming

The caption said “every edge runs both ways.” That was false, and I should have caught it earlier. Kierkegaard is dead. Clark and Chalmers do not know you exist. MUBI does not change because you watched something. Of eleven edges, exactly two are genuinely bidirectional — the feed (your attention is a real input to a recommender that then changes what it shows you and others) and the machines, weakly. The rest are one-way influence that feels reciprocal because reading someone changes how you read them.

fixed on the siteThe caption now reads: “most of these run one way. two of them run both — and that difference is the whole story.” The honest version is the sharper argument, because it isolates exactly what makes a feed different from a book.

fig. 4 · precedenceaspiration

Coherent as a manifesto, not true as a description. Nobody runs a decision tree; you act and then reconstruct the reason. The footnote (“deterministic here, messier in production”) is doing nearly all the honesty work on this figure. And the strongest word on it is the shakiest: “always.” Kindness always outranking rigor is a real ethical position, but it collides with your own principles — dissect the package, impact over intent, question everything — in every case where the kind move is the uninformative one.

worth decidingEither keep “always” and own it as a chosen commitment rather than a derived result, or change that edge to “first” — kindness gets the first word, not necessarily the last. One word, and the tree stops contradicting its neighbours.

fig. 5 · directionally correctmetaphor with a hole

The dynamics are honest — inputs bend rather than block, the target drifts and says so, the field never empties, there is no win state. But there is a real conceptual hole: the bricks are pre-labeled. Green is signal, violet is noise, and you know which is which before you hit them. In actual epistemics that is precisely what you don’t get. As built, the game is about obeying a known evidence hierarchy, not about discernment.

the fix that would make it trueGrey the bricks until impact and reveal signal or noise only after the ball hits — you learn what an input was worth after it has already bent you. That is the real experience, and it would make fig. 5 the second-most honest figure instead of the prettiest.

the meta-claim · “five lenses, the residue is the self”authored, not discovered

The defensible core is standard and true: different formalisms capture different structure, and no single model of a person is adequate. The flourish — that what persists across all five coordinate systems is the self — is rhetoric. The red dot recurs in every figure because it was drawn that way, not because reality forced it. The figures cohere because they were built to cohere.

why it still standsAuthored coherence is design, not fraud — as long as nobody claims it was found rather than made. The page never says the diagrams were derived from data, and the ledger bets against the design surviving twelve months. That is the right amount of doubt to leave in public.

Net: the site is honest about being a designed argument rather than a set of measurements, and its one genuinely quantitative claim is the one that was actually verified. Two fixes remain open — the geometry disclaimer and hidden brick types — plus one decision that is yours: whether kindness gets the first word or the last.

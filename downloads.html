some tools i build and use to be more intentional, curate my internet, and remove noise.

kan: a small assistant that runs on your own computer and answers from your own notes. EU-made models, about 2.2 GB, no internet once installed. MIT.

niwa: my rationalist thinking tool. i’m a chronic recursive overthinker; i built this to help me structure, reduce, and move toward directional clarity. no AI: clear frameworks, and creative visualisation to activate affect.

no eating what you don’t cook: the “normal diet” is steeped in corporate self-interest, ultra-processed food and invisible incentives. i only eat what i cook: fruit and vegetables, as local as possible, raw or al dente. two weeks off ultra-processed food and added sugar in march 2026 rewired my cravings; now i crave the freshness of a zucchini, the unadulterated scent of fresh parsley. just my lived experience. i’ve extended the same ethos to my attention diet: a one-click browser extension, built on Pangram, that blocks machine-generated text and images as i browse, so i pay attention only to what matters. the ethos: if the creator used AI, they cared less than someone who gave it their own time, cognition, expression and perception. on by default.

cognitive scaffolding: tools for structured overthinking, so the thinking ends in something done. runs in your browser; the ledger stays there.

kiku: my regulated information conduit. i consume exactly what i’ve chosen, as text, audio or video, converted freely between them, then cohered with what i already know, so every unit of attention is additive, multiplicative or clarifying, never compulsive time-filling. a small protest against the forced commodification of attention.

brian eno’s oblique strategies: which i find very helpful for an intentional reframe when i’m creatively stuck. 30-second DIY version: your own cards, from your creative values.
